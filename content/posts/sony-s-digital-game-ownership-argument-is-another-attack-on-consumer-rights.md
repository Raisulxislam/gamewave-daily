## Sony's Digital Ownership Defense Should Concern Every Gamer

Sony has taken a deeply controversial position in an ongoing class-action lawsuit: the company argues that it does not mislead customers when it sells digital games as licenses rather than actual ownership of the product. In other words, when you "buy" a game on PlayStation, Sony says you clearly understand you are not buying the game itself.

This is not just a legal technicality. It is a foundational argument about the future of game ownership, and it sets a troubling precedent as the industry pushes aggressively toward an all-digital landscape.

## The Bigger Fight Over What "Buying" a Game Means

The distinction between owning a product and licensing it has enormous implications for consumer rights — including what happens when servers shut down, stores close, or terms of service change. Sony's position essentially asks courts to validate that a license is an acceptable substitute for ownership, even when consumers reasonably believe they are purchasing something permanent.

As Sony continues marching toward an all-digital future, this lawsuit could define whether players ever truly own their digital libraries — or merely rent them at the publisher's discretion.
