Nobody hands money back in the games business, which is exactly why Panic's latest move deserves attention. The handheld console maker — best known for the quirky Playdate — is refunding the US tariff fees it had passed along to its customers, with a refreshingly simple explanation: "it's just not our money to keep."

## Panic is paying the tariff refund forward

As the US government sends out tariff refunds of its own, Panic has decided the responsible thing is to route that money back to the people who actually paid it. Tariffs have quietly inflated hardware prices across the industry lately, and brands have generally treated those extra fees as just another cost of doing business. Panic's choice to reverse them stands out precisely because it was under no visible obligation to do so.

## A small gesture with an outsized message

It is a modest sum across the industry's grand scheme, but the principle lands hard. When a company shrugs and says the money was never theirs, it draws a sharp line against quietly pocketing surcharges. For Panic's fans, it is also a reminder of why they trust the brand in the first place — and for everyone else, a rare feel-good story in a week full of fine print.
