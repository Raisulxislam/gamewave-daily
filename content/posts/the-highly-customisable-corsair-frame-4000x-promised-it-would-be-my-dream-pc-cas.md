Corsair's Frame 4000X arrived on the scene with an ambitious pitch: a highly modular, deeply customisable PC case that could be whatever build you wanted it to be. On paper, the concept is genuinely appealing — more configuration options should mean more freedom for builders to dial in exactly the setup they want.

## Plenty of Parts, But Does the Whole Add Up?

The case ships with a generous array of modular components, and there's no denying the sheer variety on offer. Panels, mounting points, fan configurations — the Frame 4000X gives you more raw building material than most mid-towers in its price range. But customisation without coherence is just complexity, and that's where early impressions get more measured.

Some reviewers have noted that while the individual parts feel well-made, the experience of actually putting them together doesn't always flow as smoothly as Corsair's marketing suggests. The promise of a dream case is a high bar, and the Frame 4000X is still working to clear it — though it remains one of the more interesting chassis concepts to land in recent memory.
