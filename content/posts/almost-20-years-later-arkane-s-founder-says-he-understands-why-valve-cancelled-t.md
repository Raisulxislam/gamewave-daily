For almost 20 years, the Half-Life sequel that never shipped has been known simply as Ravenholm, and its legend has only grown in the silence. Arkane Studios built the original prototype under Valve's roof in an era when both studios were smaller, stranger and more like-minded — and now Arkane founder Raphaël Colantonio has finally explained why it never saw release.

## Why Valve Pulled the Plug on Ravenholm

The short answer, in his telling, is that it all came down to the economics. A single-player spin-off that would likely sell a fraction of what the mainline franchise moves is a hard pitch even when the source material is beloved, and the prototype carried real money without a clear path to shipping. Colantonio frames the cancellation less as betrayal, more as arithmetic.

## A Sad But Familiar Math

The sting is that Ravenholm's reputation is built entirely on what never existed. Two decades of free-floating 'imagine if' enthusiasm is exactly the energy modern publishers now chase with remasters and remakes — proof, perhaps, that Valve's spreadsheets and our imagination were never doing the same calculation.
