If you shelled out extra for a PlayStation or Xbox earlier this year because of President Trump's sweeping tariff plan, don't hold your breath waiting for a refund check. Both Sony and Microsoft have now made the same argument in court filings: they are under no legal obligation to return money to consumers who absorbed tariff-related price increases on their consoles.

The two gaming giants are far from alone in this stance. Nintendo blazed the trail earlier with nearly identical legal reasoning, and now the Big Three console makers are singing from the same hymn sheet. The collective position is striking in its bluntness — **the added cost of doing business during a tariff escalation is the customer's problem, not the platform holder's**.

Consumer advocacy groups have pushed back, arguing that price hikes passed down from tariffs effectively amount to a hidden surcharge buyers never consented to. Courts will ultimately decide whether that framing holds water, but for the millions of players who upgraded their hardware at elevated price points, the message from the industry is unmistakably clear: you paid the premium, and you're keeping it.
