It is easy to imagine id Software as the scrappy outlier that would have batted generative AI away. The studio that built Doom and Quake has always carried a purist, code-first mystique. So it lands as a genuine surprise that one of its former co-owners now says, plainly, that they would have gone all in on gen AI back in the day.

## Why the 'Half-Life 2 of AI' remark hits home

The comment is not just nostalgia talking. It frames the whole debate the right way: generative tools are only as good as the bar set for them. The ex-co-owner's own joke — that someone needs to build the "Half-Life 2 of AI" — is really a way of saying the tech is still waiting for its masterpiece. In that framing, id and its generation were never the audience that would refuse the tools. They were the kind of studio that would have pushed them into something that mattered.

## A reminder to judge the tools, not the era

The revelation is a useful corrective. Generative AI in game development is too often framed as a betrayal of a golden, artisanal past — as if legends of the craft would have refused it on principle. This admission argues otherwise, and argues it from a voice few can dismiss. The technology deserves the same scrutiny as any tool, but it also deserves a fair hearing instead of a reflexive no.
