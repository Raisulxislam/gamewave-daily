## The 10-Year-Old Game GTA 6 Has to Beat

Grand Theft Auto 6 is coming with a mountain of expectations, but a Valve veteran argues the most dangerous obstacle isn't a rival studio — it's Rockstar's own success. The biggest question looming over the sequel is how its multiplayer, GTA 6 Online, can possibly pull players away from the decade-old GTA Online that millions still can't quit.

It's a fair point and a counterintuitive one. GTA Online has been a cash cow and a cultural institution since 2013, quietly surviving three console generations and building a player base whose progress, property, and friendships are all locked into a single game. Asking them to start over is asking a lot.

## Success Is the Hardest Metric to Beat

Rockstar's plans for GTA 6's post-launch online world remain the mystery at the heart of the sequel. History suggests the studio recognizes the risk. Past Rockstar online launches — from Red Dead Online to the various GTA updates — show the company knows how to iterate, but also how easily a successor can feel like a reboot nobody asked for. The player base is the moat, and the moat cuts both ways.

The veteran's skepticism is worth taking seriously. For the millions currently grinding through Los Santos, the question isn't whether GTA 6 Online will be good. It's whether "good" is enough to convince them to walk away from years of invested time. Grand Theft Auto 6 may be the biggest game ever made, but its truest test may be whether its online future can make players believe the old world is worth leaving behind.
