Zach Cregger wants his **Resident Evil** film adaptation to stand on its own feet — even if that means leaving some of the franchise's most outlandish lore at the door. The Barbarian director told fans he has no interest in cramming every monster and mythology thread from the games into a single movie.

## Staying Grounded in a Franchise Known for Going Big

“I’m not going to have a gigantic jacked dude in a trench coat,” Cregger said, seemingly steering clear of Chris Redfield’s more extreme on-screen moments and the series’ tendency toward bombastic set pieces. His point is straightforward: the films that work best pick a lane and commit to it, rather than trying to service every corner of a sprawling canon.

## A Director’s Instinct Over a Checklist

Cregger’s approach signals a potential shift in how Hollywood handles horror game IPs. Instead of fan-service overload, he appears focused on tone and character — the elements that turned his 2022 debut into a genuine word-of-mouth hit. For long-time **Resident Evil** fans, the real question is whether trimming the wilder edges will strengthen the film or strip away exactly what makes the franchise so enjoyable in the first place.
