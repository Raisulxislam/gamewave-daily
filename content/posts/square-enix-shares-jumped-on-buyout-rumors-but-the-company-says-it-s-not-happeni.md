## Privatization Rumor Sends Square Enix Stock Soaring

A Japanese monthly magazine report suggesting Square Enix Holdings was considering going private sent the company's stock jumping nearly 7% on September 1 — even before the company issued a denial. The report, published in the September issue of *Sentaku* under the headline "Privatization Speculation Surrounds Square Enix, Foreign Funds Stirred," triggered immediate market reaction on the Tokyo Stock Exchange, with shares closing at 3,161 yen.

Square Enix responded with a timely disclosure titled "Regarding Certain Media Reports," stating plainly that the report "was not announced by Square Enix" and that the company "has not considered privatization internally." It's as definitive a denial as you'll get from a publicly traded Japanese firm.

## Why the Rumor Refused to Die

The speculation wasn't entirely baseless. Singapore-based investment fund 3D Investment Partners disclosed an 18.53% stake in Square Enix in March — a 1.03 percentage point increase from its previous filing. Privatization trends are also on the rise globally and in Japan, driven by companies tired of activist investor pressure and quarterly earnings scrutiny. The founding Fukushima family remains a major shareholder, which theoretically gives management the leverage to go private without a hostile fight.

Still, Square Enix's flat denial and the absence of any prior signals — no insider-trading-style accumulation before the report broke — suggest the market got ahead of itself. Investors who bought into the rumor on the way up may now face an uncomfortable correction. The lesson: a stock price spike fueled by a magazine article and a denial the same day is a textbook case of rumor-driven volatility, not a signal of strategic intent.
