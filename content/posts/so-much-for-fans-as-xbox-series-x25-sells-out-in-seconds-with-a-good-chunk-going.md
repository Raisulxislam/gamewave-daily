## Another Preorder Disaster

Microsoft's limited-edition Xbox Series X25 went up for preorder on August 27, and if you were a genuine fan hoping to secure one, chances are you walked away empty-handed. The console sold out within seconds across major retailers, and community feedback quickly turned sour as buyers reported site crashes, queue resets, and inventory vanishing before their browsers could even load the checkout page.

Worse, early resale listings began appearing on platforms like eBay within minutes — some at double or triple the retail price. The pattern is familiar to anyone who has tried to buy a high-demand console or graphics card in the past five years: automated bots scoop up inventory faster than a human can click, and the people who actually want to play the machine are left paying a premium on the secondary market.

## Microsoft's Scalper Problem Is Everyone's Scalper Problem

It is worth noting that Microsoft has made efforts in the past to combat scalper purchasing, including purchase limits and verification steps. None of it appears to have fully solved the problem for this launch, which suggests the issue is less about one company's checkout system and more about an industry-wide failure to protect retail access for ordinary buyers.
