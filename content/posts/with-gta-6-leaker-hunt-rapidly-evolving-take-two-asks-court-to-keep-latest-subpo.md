Take-Two Interactive has filed an urgent motion to keep its latest subpoena request under wraps, arguing that public disclosure could hand GTA 6's leakers a roadmap for covering their tracks. The filing, submitted to a federal court, describes the ongoing investigation as "rapidly evolving" and warns that even partial transparency at this stage could compromise the entire effort.

## What's actually at stake

The subpoena itself targets information that Take-Two's lawyers say is "highly sensitive" and central to identifying the individuals responsible for one of the most damaging leaks in gaming history. The original GTA 6 footage — hundreds of clips of early build gameplay — hit the internet in September 2022 and triggered a legal firestorm that has dragged on for nearly four years. At the time, the leak was attributed to a teenage hacker linked to the Lapsus$ group, but the scope of the investigation has clearly widened well beyond that initial arrest.

## Why sealing matters

Take-Two's argument is straightforward: if the leakers learn which accounts, devices, or communications the subpoena covers, they can destroy evidence before investigators get to it. The company is asking the court to treat the request with the same secrecy typically reserved for national-security matters — a sign of just how seriously Rockstar's parent company is taking this. For a game that won't arrive until at least 2026, the legal chess match is already one of the more fascinating subplots in the industry.
