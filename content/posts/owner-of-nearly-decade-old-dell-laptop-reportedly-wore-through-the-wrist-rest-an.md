There's wearing in, and then there's wearing *through*. A Reddit user has posted images of a nearly ten-year-old Dell laptop whose wrist rest area has been completely eroded by years of friction — exposing the raw carbon-fiber structure underneath.

## When Daily Use Becomes Structural Damage

The user describes the sensation as being "stabbed by carbon fibers" with every session. The palm rest, once smooth and uniform, now shows a ragged hole where skin and pressure have slowly eaten through the top layer. It's a striking visual reminder that even well-built machines have physical limits, and that a decade of daily use can turn a cosmetic issue into a genuine comfort problem.

## A Viral Thread and a Common Problem

The post quickly drew a flood of responses from other long-term Dell owners sharing similar experiences. While carbon fiber is prized for its strength-to-weight ratio in laptop chassis design, it turns out that repeated abrasion from palms and wrists can compromise the surface layer over time. For now, the original poster appears to be continuing to use the laptop — albeit with considerably more wince than before.
