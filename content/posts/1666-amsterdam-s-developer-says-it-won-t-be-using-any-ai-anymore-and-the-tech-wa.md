## AI Out, Human Touch In

Patrice Désilets, the creative mind behind the upcoming *1666: Amsterdam*, has confirmed that AI has been "totally removed" from the studio's development pipeline. The revelation came as a surprising admission from a studio that, by its own account, leaned on AI tools during the earliest stages of the project — precisely the phase where creative identity takes shape.

The tech was reportedly used only in "conception and pre-production," which raises an interesting question about AI's real utility in game development. If the most creative phase of the entire process is where AI served its purpose, what does that say about the tool's limitations once actual implementation begins?

### Why Conception Matters Most

Pre-production is where concepts are born, art styles are defined, and narrative DNA is established. Having AI embedded at that stage, only to strip it away later, suggests the technology may have been useful for rapid ideation but fell short when the studio needed consistent, handcrafted execution. It's a pragmatic approach — use the tool where it helps, discard it where it doesn't — but it also signals that even forward-thinking studios aren't finding lasting value in AI beyond the brainstorming table.
