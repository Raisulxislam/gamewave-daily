Xbox CEO Asha Sharma has waded into one of gaming's most heated consumer debates — and done it with notable restraint. In a recent BBC interview, Sharma was asked directly about the backlash Sony has faced over rumours that the next PlayStation may ship without a disc drive. Her answer was diplomatic: she acknowledged that "players love physical media" and that the desire to own a tangible copy of a game remains strong, even as digital distribution continues its march toward dominance.

## What she didn't say

Crucially, Sharma offered no commitment about Xbox's own next-generation console, widely referred to as Helix. The next-gen Xbox, rumoured to be in advanced development with a target launch in 2027, is widely expected to be a major departure from the current Series X architecture. Whether it will include a disc drive — or follow Sony's reported lead and go fully digital — remains one of the biggest unanswered questions in the console space.

## The physical media dilemma

Sharma's carefully worded response highlights a genuine tension in the console market. Physical media enthusiasts represent a passionate and vocal minority — they buy games, display collections, and resist the subscription-model future that both Microsoft and Sony are increasingly pushing. But they're also a shrinking segment of the overall market. Xbox has been more aggressive than Sony about digital-first releases and Game Pass, making Sharma's acknowledgment of physical media's appeal feel less like a promise and more like an empathetic nod before the inevitable shift continues.
