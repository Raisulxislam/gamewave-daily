Dell has unveiled the Dell 14S — and it is not an XPS. The company is deliberately distancing its latest 14-inch Windows 11 machine from its premium brand, positioning it as an accessible, everyday competitor to Apple's MacBook Neo.

## What the Dell 14S Brings to the Table

The laptop ships in multiple colour options, a lightweight chassis designed around portability, and a battery rated for a full working day. Under the hood, Dell is betting that most users do not need cutting-edge silicon — they need a machine that is fast enough, light enough, and does not die before 5 p.m. That pragmatic positioning undercuts the MacBook Neo on price while matching Apple on comfort.

## Why Ditching the XPS Name Matters

By stepping away from the XPS badge, Dell avoids the expectation of premium-tier pricing and performance — and that gives the 14S room to compete on pure value. Apple's MacBook Neo set a new bar for what an affordable ultraportable should feel like; Dell's answer is to hit the same notes while undercutting it on sticker price.

Whether the 14S can steal meaningful market share from Apple's MacBooks will depend on real-world reviews, but Dell's willingness to launch a new brand line shows the company is serious about reclaiming the mid-range laptop conversation.
