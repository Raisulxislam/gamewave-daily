## CD Projekt Red Confronts Its Biggest Misstep

Years after one of the most notorious game launches in modern history, CD Projekt Red has publicly acknowledged that releasing Cyberpunk 2077 in its broken December 2020 state was, in their own words, "for sure wrong."

The admission carries weight precisely because of how long it took. Cyberpunk 2077's launch was a catastrophe by any standard — the game shipped riddled with bugs and performance issues so severe that Sony took the extraordinary step of pulling it from the PlayStation Store entirely, a move almost unheard of for a major AAA title. CD Projekt Red spent the better part of three years rebuilding goodwill through patches, free DLC, and the Phantom Liberty expansion before the studio's reputation began to meaningfully recover.

## What the Admission Actually Signals

The phrasing matters. "Was for sure wrong" is about as close to an unreserved corporate apology as the gaming industry tends to see. CD Projekt Red has walked a fine line between acknowledging the problems and defending its creative ambitions ever since the launch. This direct statement suggests the company has moved past the defensive posture it maintained through the worst of the backlash.

It's also a reminder that the industry still lacks meaningful accountability mechanisms for botched launches. Cyberpunk 2077 went on to sell over 30 million copies — a figure that confirms both the depth of the initial disappointment and the remarkable recovery that followed. For CD Projekt Red, the hard-won lesson appears to be that transparency and sustained effort can undo even catastrophic first impressions, but that doesn't make the original decision any less damaging.
