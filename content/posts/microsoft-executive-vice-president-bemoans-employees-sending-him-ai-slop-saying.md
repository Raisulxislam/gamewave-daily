## The Irony Is Thick Enough to Cut With a Katana

Ryan Roslansky, Microsoft's executive vice president overseeing Office and LinkedIn, recently took to social media to vent about employees flooding his inbox with AI-generated content. "This is a doom loop," he wrote, describing the frustration of sorting through machine-written summaries and reports that nobody asked for.

## When Your Own Tools Become the Problem

The self-own is remarkable. Microsoft has spent the last two years aggressively pushing Copilot and AI features into every corner of its productivity suite — Word, PowerPoint, Outlook, Teams. The company literally incentivized employees to use these tools. Now its own executive is publicly complaining about the result. It's like selling someone a megaphone and then griping about the noise.

The broader lesson here isn't about one executive's inbox. It's about what happens when AI tools are deployed without clear purpose. Generative AI is powerful when applied to specific, well-defined problems. When it becomes a default action — "just let Copilot handle it" — the output tends to be mediocre, and everyone suffers, including the people who built the tools in the first place.
