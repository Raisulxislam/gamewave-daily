Xbox Cloud Gaming has pulled the rug out from under one of its most popular perks. Starting in November, Game Pass subscribers will face monthly hour caps on cloud streaming — a stark reversal after Microsoft expanded unlimited cloud access to all tiers less than a year ago.

## The Numbers Behind the Backlash

Game Pass Ultimate subscribers will get 15 hours per month, Premium users will see 10, and Essential subscribers will be limited to a meagre 5 hours of cloud playtime. Once those hours are burned through, players will need to purchase additional time through the Xbox Store — pricing details remain unannounced. Microsoft says the change impacts roughly 4% of its subscriber base.

## A Betrayal of the Cloud Gaming Promise?

The official reasoning is straightforward: the cost of cloud infrastructure scales with usage, and limits allow Microsoft to keep investing in reliability and performance. But the optics are brutal. Unlimited cloud gaming was a headline selling point when it rolled out to Premium and Essential tiers, and yanking it back now feels less like a business necessity and more like a bait-and-switch. Non-subscribers will also gain the option to buy cloud hours for games they already own, which reads less as consumer-friendly and more as the opening move toward a usage-based billing model that nobody asked for.
