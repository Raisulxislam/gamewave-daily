Nvidia still hasn't put a date on DLSS 5 — the neural rendering overhaul that drew immediate backlash when it was previewed earlier this year. But that hasn't stopped the modding community from getting their hands on it anyway.

A leaked build of the technology is now circulating among PC modders, who have figured out how to inject it into virtually any game. What they've found is... not pretty. The AI-powered face filters, intended to enhance character models in real time, are producing uncanny distortions that range from mildly unsettling to genuinely nightmarish. Characters' features warp unpredictably, with eyes, jaws, and skin textures morphing into something no renderer should be proud of.

## The Core Problem With AI-Driven Rendering

The whole point of DLSS 5's neural approach is to let AI reconstruct and enhance visuals beyond what traditional upscaling can achieve. But when the model doesn't have enough context — or encounters character designs it wasn't trained on — the results speak for themselves, and they're not saying anything good.

This leak doesn't confirm a release timeline, and Nvidia may well refine the technology before it ships. But it's a stark reminder that AI-enhanced graphics remain a work in progress, and the gap between a controlled keynote demo and real-world gameplay is still很大 enough to drive a truck through.
