If you were planning a PC build or upgrade, brace yourself. Motherboard manufacturers are facing steep increases in component pricing, with some parts jumping as much as 50 percent. The surge is driven by rising costs across capacitors, PCB materials, and controller chips — parts that don't make headlines the way GPUs do, but quietly underpin every board on the shelf.

The timing couldn't be worse. We're in the middle of a generational hardware transition, with new chipsets and platform launches rolling out across both AMD and Intel. Board partners who absorbed cost increases during the last cycle are now showing less appetite to eat those margins again.

## What This Means for PC Builders

For consumers, the practical effect is straightforward: expect higher MSRPs on mid-range and enthusiast boards. Entry-level models may stay relatively stable for now, but the segments most builders actually want — with robust VRMs, multiple M.2 slots, and WiFi 7 — are squarely in the crosshairs. **PC builders on a budget should seriously consider locking in current-gen boards now rather than waiting.**

## The Bigger Picture

Component cost volatility is becoming a recurring theme in the PC hardware space. Between memory pricing swings, NAND fluctuations, and now board-level increases, the total cost of a thoughtful build keeps drifting upward. It's a reminder that the GPU is never the only line item that moves.
