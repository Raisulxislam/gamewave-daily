The Elder Scrolls 6 exclusivity question has become the industry's favourite party trick: everyone asks, nobody answers. This week at Gamescom, the BBC confronted Xbox CEO Asha Sharma with the question that has dogged Microsoft since it acquired Bethesda in 2021 — will TES6 come to PlayStation? Sharma's response was a masterclass in saying everything and nothing: Microsoft could make more exclusives, she conceded, but she declined to specify whether The Elder Scrolls VI would be one of them.

## The Bethesda question that won't go away

Ever since the $7.5 billion acquisition closed, every Microsoft earnings call and games showcase has been shadowed by one unspoken question: what's the point of owning Bethesda if you don't use it to sell Xbox consoles? Microsoft's answer so far has been ambiguous at best. Titles like Starfield launched as Xbox and PC exclusives, but subsequent releases — including the much-loved Hi-Fi Rush — have found homes on PlayStation, muddying the picture considerably.

## Why the BBC question matters

Sharma's Gamescom comments landed in a particularly sensitive window. Xbox's multiplatform push has boosted revenue but alarmed the console-loyal fanbase, while PlayStation players are watching closely to see whether Microsoft will truly leverage its biggest franchise as a platformDifferentiator. By refusing to tip her hand, Sharma keeps both camps engaged and forces Sony to plan for every scenario. Whether the Elder Scrolls VI ultimately lands on PlayStation is almost secondary at this point — the fact that Microsoft can credibly threaten to withhold it is itself a form of power.
