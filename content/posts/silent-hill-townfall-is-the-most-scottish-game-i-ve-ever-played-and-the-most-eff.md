Silent Hill: Townfall is the most Scottish game I've ever played — and, without exaggeration, the most effective horror I've sat down with in years. Developer No Code hasn't simply set its Silent Hill in Scotland; it has built the whole nightmare out of the country's weather, coastlines and folklore.

The Glasgow studio behind Observation says the choice was personal. "It made sense for us to dig deep into our upbringings, into our past, and try and build a place that felt believable."

## Silent Hill: Townfall finds its horror in Scotland

What makes it work is how naturally the setting folds into the dread. Storm-swept coastal towns, uneasy small communities and a landscape that feels watched — the unease settles in gradually and never quite leaves.

## A Silent Hill that earns its scares

Too many horror games rely on cheap jump scares to do the heavy lifting. Townfall instead lets the discomfort accumulate, so the fear feels earned rather than purchased. If this tone holds across the whole game, Townfall could give the franchise its most distinct identity in years.
