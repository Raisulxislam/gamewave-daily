## A Combat System That Finally Clicks

I went hands-on with Fable at Gamescom this week, and I'll cut to the chase: Playground Games has built the best-feeling combat in the franchise's history. A 30-minute demo set in an abandoned mine called "Mud and Death" let me swing a two-handed Bower Hammer, fire a Deepwood Bow, and chain spells like Lift, Lightning, and the gloriously on-brand Befowl — which turns enemies into chickens you can then punt across the screen.

The key innovation is seamless switching. Hold the right trigger and press a face button to cast a spell; release it and you're back to melee. There's no menu fiddling, no loadout bar. You just fight, and the system flows. My two playthroughs with different builds — one damage-focused, one crowd-control with a repeater crossbow and levitation — showed how dramatically loadouts reshape the experience.

## The Verdict: Impressive, But the Real Test Is September

The Lantern Keeper mini-boss, a root-and-branch abomination with a lantern for a head, capped the demo with a multi-phase fight that demanded parrying, ranged precision, and spell combos all at once. It felt like a genuine power fantasy. Whether the full open world and story deliver is another question, but on pure combat mechanics, this is a **Fable worth waiting for**.

The catch? This game launches February 23, 2027 on Xbox Series X\|S, PC, and PS5 — and every reveal faces the so-called Xbox Tax: a reflexive online skepticism reserved for anything branded green. If the demo is any indication, that tax may finally be unjustified.
