## The Human Cost Behind Doom: The Dark Ages

A former id Software producer has spoken publicly about what they describe as the most severe crunch period in the history of the Doom franchise, saying the push to ship Doom: The Dark Ages took a devastating personal toll.

"There were two months where I was working something like at least 12 hours every day," the producer recounted. "There were days where I didn't see my son." The comments paint a stark picture of the conditions behind one of the most anticipated first-person shooters of recent years — a game that earned widespread critical praise upon release but clearly came at a steep human price.

## Crunch Culture in the AAA Spotlight

The producer did not mince words about the intensity, calling it "the worst crunch in the series' history" — a significant statement given that the Doom franchise spans decades and multiple studio reboots. Previous Doom entries, particularly the 2016 reboot and Doom Eternal in 2020, were also associated with reports of extended overtime, but this account suggests the pressure on The Dark Ages exceeded all prior benchmarks.

The remarks arrive at a moment when crunch continues to be one of the gaming industry's most contentious labor issues. Despite growing public awareness and occasional policy announcements from publishers, first-hand accounts like this one underscore that the problem remains deeply embedded in AAA development cycles. For id Software, a studio with an outsized reputation built on creative ambition, the question now is whether the lessons of this crunch will translate into structural change.
