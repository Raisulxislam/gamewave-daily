There's a particular kind of irony in being asked to explain why your own products are getting more expensive — and Xbox CEO Asha Sharma found herself confronting exactly that this week. The price of an Xbox console today is higher than it has ever been, a trend driven by a confluence of factors including supply-chain disruptions, component shortages, and the broader semiconductor crunch. But one factor looms larger than the rest: Microsoft's own aggressive push into artificial intelligence.

## The AI cost pipeline

Microsoft has committed tens of billions of dollars to AI infrastructure — data centres, custom chips, GPU clusters — in a bid to dominate the emerging market. That spending has tightened the global supply of high-end silicon, directly inflating the cost of the components that go into every Xbox, PlayStation, and gaming PC. It's a feedback loop that Sharma didn't create, but one that Microsoft's boardroom decisions have undeniably accelerated.

## What Sharma actually said

In her BBC interview, Sharma acknowledged the tension with characteristic poise: the rising cost of hardware is real, she said, and Microsoft is aware of the pressure it places on consumers. But she stopped short of linking that cost to the company's own AI investments — a conspicuously diplomatic omission. For Xbox fans watching their favourite platform become more expensive by the generation, the CEO's words may ring hollow without concrete action on pricing.
