## The Rollable Laptop Finally Gets Serious

I've grown accustomed to rolling my eyes at Lenovo's concept machines. The foldable PC, the dual-screen experiment, the rollable display prototypes — ambitious, clever, and almost always a solution in search of a problem. Then I spent time with Project Swan, and something changed my mind.

This rollable laptop concept genuinely feels like the first one a real person might actually put on a credit card. Hinges are the weak point of most portables, and Swan's rollable display sidesteps them in the smartest way yet — unfurling more screen when you need it and tucking neatly away when you don't.

## A Concept the Market Might Just Accept

It's still a concept, of course, and concepts don't have to survive contact with a luggage carousel. But the thinking feels right this time. Instead of asking which novel form factor can justify itself, Project Swan starts with a practical question: how do you get a bigger display into a smaller body without the hinge? Answering it that way is why this machine feels less like theatre and more like a product.

After years of Lenovo grand designs that looked wonderful in press shots and wobbled in reality, the rollable laptop concept finally lands on a pitch that doesn't feel like science fiction. The company's smartest approach yet — and the first one I'd actually consider buying.
