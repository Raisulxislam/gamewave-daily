The live-service graveyard is littered with ambitious projects that burned budgets and goodwill before being quietly switched off — so announcing a brand-new live-service MOBA in this climate takes genuine nerve. Yet here we are: a new challenger has surfaced anyway, and honestly, I'm shocked. Also, a little curious.

The odds are brutal. The genre's throne has been held for well over a decade by League of Legends and Dota 2, and that pair has buried a generation of would-be rivals. That makes any fresh live-service MOBA announcement feel less like ambition and more like a dare.

## Why a live-service MOBA is the riskiest bet in gaming

Even studios with giant budgets have watched their always-online games close within months, so a newcomer needs something the genre hasn't already handed to players. Depth alone won't cut it.

## The upside if it actually lands

Here's what keeps me interested: the genre survives precisely because its best games keep pulling players back. If this new game pairs real depth with a softer learning curve and smarter session lengths, it might find the audience everyone else forgot to serve. It's a long shot. But after all these graves, it's nice to see someone aiming for the living.
