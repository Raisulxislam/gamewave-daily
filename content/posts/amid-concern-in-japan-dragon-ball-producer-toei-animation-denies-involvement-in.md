## Toei Steps Back From the Announcement

When Saudi Arabia and France jointly announced a Dragon Ball theme park destined for the Paris region last week, the gaming and anime world collectively did a double-take. The surprise reveal triggered a tidal wave of commentary online — and a sizable chunk of it, written in Japanese, zeroed in on an uncomfortable question: who actually signed off on this?

## Licensing Rights and Japanese Representation

Toei Animation, the studio behind the beloved franchise and a key stakeholder in the Dragon Ball brand, moved quickly to distance itself. In a statement widely picked up by Japanese outlets, the company said it has no involvement whatsoever in the proposed park. That clarification matters. Fans and industry observers alike had been voicing concern that the project was proceeding without meaningful Japanese participation, raising red flags about licensing agreements and creative oversight.

**Here's the reality check: a major Dragon Ball theme park concept is apparently moving forward without the blessing — or even the participation — of the animation studio most closely identified with the franchise.** That is an unusual position for any IP partnership, and it explains why the response in Japan has been more anxious than excited. Whether Toei's absence is a contractual footnote or a genuine rift remains to be seen, but the story is far from over.
