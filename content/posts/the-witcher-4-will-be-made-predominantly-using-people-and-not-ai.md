In an industry increasingly scrambled by the question of where — and how much — artificial intelligence should factor into game development, CD Projekt has drawn a clear line. Joint-CEO Michał Nowakowski has confirmed that The Witcher 4 will be made "predominantly using people," putting human artistry ahead of algorithmic shortcuts.

The statement lands at a moment when virtually every major publisher is grappling with AI's role in their pipelines, from asset generation to QA testing. **CD Projekt has previously stated that it would not use AI as a replacement for its workforce**, and Nowakowski's comments reinforce that the studio views human talent as a core competitive advantage, not a cost center to be optimized away.

Whether this pledge survives contact with the realities of modern AAA budgets and timelines remains an open question — but for now, the Witcher franchise's next chapter is being written by hands, not prompts.
