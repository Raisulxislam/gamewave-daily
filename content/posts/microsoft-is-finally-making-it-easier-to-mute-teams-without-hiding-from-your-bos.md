## Muting Teams without the guilt

If you've ever silenced Teams notifications and then panicked when a colleague sees the red Do Not Disturb dot, Microsoft finally has a fix. A new Quiet Mode feature, rolling out in the coming weeks, lets users temporarily pause all incoming notifications — without broadcasting to your entire organization that you've gone dark.

The difference from Do Not Disturb is deliberate and important. DND has always carried a social signal: *this person is unavailable and doesn't want to talk.* Quiet Mode, by contrast, is invisible. Colleagues won't see any status change. Messages still arrive; they just don't ping, ring, or vibrate until you're ready.

## Why this small change matters

**It's a concession that work culture hasn't caught up to remote tools.** In theory, Do Not Disturb should be a perfectly normal boundary. In practice, it reads as a statement — especially in environments where responsiveness is quietly equated with commitment. Quiet Mode sidesteps that entire dynamic.

The feature is simple: a single toggle in the notification settings, with a configurable timer. No status change, no calendar override, no awkward explanations. For a tool that hundreds of millions of people live inside for eight-plus hours a day, this kind of ergonomic thinking is overdue.
