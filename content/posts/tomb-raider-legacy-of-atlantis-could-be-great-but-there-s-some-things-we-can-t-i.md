The latest look at *Tomb Raider: Legacy of Atlantis* revealed a game with real ambition — lush environments, a more open exploration structure, and a Lara Croft who feels closer to the survivalist of the 2013 reboot than the action hero of the mid-2010s. Crystal Dynamics clearly wants this to be the definitive Lara Croft experience. But buried in the details are a few design choices that give long-time fans legitimate pause.

## Skill Trees and Modern Hand-Holding

The newly revealed skill tree system divides Lara's progression into combat, exploration, and survival branches. On paper, it's a sensible modern RPG convention. In practice, it raises the question of whether Tomb Raider is drifting toward the same open-world template that every major franchise now seems obligated to follow. More concerning is the assisted navigation system — a glowing path that guides players through environments. It's optional, the developers say, but the fact that it exists at all feels like a concession to market trends rather than a genuine design conviction.

There's a tension here worth acknowledging. *Legacy of Atlantis* wants to attract new players while respecting the legacy that made Tomb Raider matter in the first place. **The potential is enormous — the setting alone, with its deep-water ruins and mythological hooks, is exactly what this franchise needs.** But if the execution leans too hard into trend-chasing, it could lose the very identity that makes Tomb Raider distinct.
