I went hands-on with God Save Birmingham at Gamescom, and I need to get something off my chest straight away: I'm from Birmingham. I grew up dodging pigeons in the Bullring, navigating the concrete jungle of the old library, and pretending Spaghetti Junction was normal. So when someone tells me they've made a zombie survival game set in my actual hometown, you'd better believe I had strong opinions before I even touched a controller.

Good news: the game mostly delivers. God Save Birmingham drops you into a medieval-flavoured version of the city and asks you to survive waves of undead with improvised weapons and a ragtag group of survivors. The tone is somewhere between dark comedy and genuine tension, and it works far better than it has any right to.

## Birmingham, But Make It Apocalyptic

The map is instantly recognisable if you know the city. Landmarks are exaggerated but faithful, and the developers have peppered the environment with local references that will make any Brummie smile. The zombie designs lean into the medieval setting with armour, shields, and surprisingly creative undead variants. **For a Gamescom debut, this shows more personality than most triple-A trailers on the show floor.**

## The Bullring on a Saturday Still Seems Worse

Jokes aside, God Save Birmingham nails the small pleasures of survival gameplay — scavenging, crafting, and the quiet panic of hearing something groan around a corner in the Bullring's empty corridors. If you're into zombie games with actual charm and a setting that isn't another generic American city, this is worth watching. It's currently slated for a release window in early 2027.
