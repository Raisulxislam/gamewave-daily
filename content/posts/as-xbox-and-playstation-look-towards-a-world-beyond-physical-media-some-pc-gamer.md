**Physical media is ending the same way it began: loudly.** Xbox is readying its disc-to-digital program, which will let players hand back their discs and keep the game as a digital licence, while PlayStation has spent a generation sliding toward the same no-disc future. Somewhere in that moment, a group of PC gamers decided to go the exact opposite way: they are burning and pressing their own game discs.

## A Two-Speed Exit for Physical Media

While the console giants treat the disc as a liability to retire, a small but vocal pocket of PC gaming is quietly reviving it. Building a personal disc library was never really part of PC gaming's identity, and yet that is exactly what some players are doing now — a mix of nostalgia, archiving instinct and a stubborn sense of ownership. It is a reminder that the march beyond physical media is not being welcomed everywhere with open arms.

## Ownership, in a Form You Can Hold

The timing is almost theatrical. One industry leans hard into streaming and digital storefronts while a corner of it rediscovers the ritual of sliding a disc into a drive. When a game becomes just a licence on a server, something tangible is lost, and the players making their own discs are staking a claim on the oldest argument there is: if I can hold it, I own it. Whether that argument survives the shift is another question entirely — but for now, the people building discs while the giants flee them are the most interesting plot in the format war.
