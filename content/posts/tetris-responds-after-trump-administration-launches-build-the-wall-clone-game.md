The official Tetris social media pages broke their silence this week after the Trump administration launched a free online game called *Build the Wall* — a puzzle game whose core mechanics bear a striking resemblance to Tetris itself. The game, which appeared on what appears to be an administration-affiliated website, has players stacking blocks to construct a border wall.

## The Resemblance Is Hard to Miss

The gameplay loop is immediately familiar: blocks fall from the top of the screen, and the player rotates and places them to complete horizontal lines. That's Tetris. That's the thing Tetris has been since Alexey Pajitnov first coded it in Moscow in 1984. The Tetris Company, which manages the brand's licensing and IP protection, clearly noticed — and responded with a pointed post that didn't name the administration directly but left little room for interpretation.

This isn't the first time a political entity has borrowed from gaming culture, but it might be the most brazen case of mechanics lifted from one of the most recognizable games in history. **The Tetris brand has historically been aggressive about protecting its IP, and this situation likely won't be an exception.**
